Phantom URL

A phantom URL is a web address an AI presents as a source that does not exist — fabricated at answer time, plausible in structure, resolving to nothing. When the fabricated address carries a brand's domain, the brand inherits the dead end: the answer looked authoritative, the click lands on a 404.
Key takeaways
  • Phantom URLs are fabricated source addresses — the AI invents a plausible path on a real domain.
  • The user experiences your broken page, not the AI's error: the brand inherits the dead end.
  • Measure from both sides: resolve AI-cited URLs, and watch server logs for 404s with AI referrers.
  • Recurring phantom paths can be converted into working entry points with a permanent redirect.

Consensus definition

Citation fabrication is a documented failure mode of generative search engines. In the first systematic audit of citation quality across four generative search engines, only 51.5% of generated sentences were fully supported by their citations, and a quarter of citations did not support the sentence they were attached to.1 Phantom URLs are the sharpest end of that spectrum: the cited address itself is invented. The model assembles a URL that matches the pattern of a brand's site architecture — right domain, plausible path — without verifying that the page exists. The result is a citation that performs authority without providing any.

rhinegold operator refinement

Rhinegold treats phantom URLs as a brand-risk signal, not a curiosity. The user who clicks a phantom URL does not blame the AI — they experience your broken page, at the exact moment the AI positioned you as the answer. Operationally this makes phantom URLs measurable on two sides: in citation-level AI monitoring (which cited addresses return 404?) and in your own server logs (which 404s arrive with AI-platform referrers?). A recurring phantom path is also actionable: if an AI repeatedly invents the same plausible address, a redirect to the real page converts a fabricated citation into a working entry point.

Operational use

Track a phantom rate alongside Citation Rate: of all URLs the AI attributes to your domain, the share that does not resolve. Review server logs for 404s with AI referrers as the ground-truth complement. High-frequency phantom paths are candidates for permanent redirects to the nearest real page.

Measurement boundary

Phantom-URL detection requires citation-level monitoring — answer-level metrics like Mention Rate cannot see it. Detection is also provider-dependent: systems differ in how much they ground answers in live retrieval versus generate from memory, so phantom rates are not comparable across platforms without noting the grounding mechanism behind each.

The most dangerous citation is the one that resolves to nothing.

Distinct from

Against Hallucination, the broader category: hallucination covers any fabricated claim about a brand, while a phantom URL is specifically a fabricated source address. Against a stale citation — a real page that has since been removed or moved. The stale citation once existed and can be redirected from knowledge; the phantom never existed and must be discovered.

Obstacles & resolutions

Invisible to answer-level monitoring
Most AI-visibility reporting counts mentions and citations without resolving the cited addresses. A brand can show a healthy citation count while a meaningful share of those citations dead-ends — the metric looks like authority, the user experience is a 404.
rhinegold resolutionResolve every cited URL as part of citation monitoring and report the phantom share next to Citation Rate. A citation metric that does not resolve its URLs overstates standing.
The error lands on the brand
Attribution is asymmetric: the AI fabricates, the brand's site returns the error page. At the most influential moment of the journey — the moment of recommendation — the brand appears broken.
rhinegold resolutionTreat high-frequency phantom paths as demand signals. A permanent redirect to the nearest real page recovers the visit; a well-built 404 page with paths to the product is the fallback for the rest.
Phantoms persist in model memory
A fabricated path that entered a model's training or caching layers can resurface long after the answering provider fixed its retrieval. Unlike a broken backlink, there is no webmaster to write to.
rhinegold resolutionMonitoring has to be continuous rather than one-off, and the redirect countermeasure has to stay in place — phantom traffic decays slowly, on the model release cycle rather than the crawl cycle.

Observed pattern in practice

Phantom URLs cluster where retrieval is weakest: long-tail prompts, niche product areas, and pages deep in a site's architecture. They also follow a site's visible URL conventions — models extrapolate patterns they have seen, which is why a consistently structured site tends to attract plausible phantoms rather than random ones. That regularity is what makes the redirect countermeasure viable.

Common mistakes

  • Treating every AI-cited URL as a real page — citation lists must be resolved, not just counted, or the phantom share silently inflates apparent authority.
  • Blaming the 404s on site migration before checking referrers — AI-fabricated paths and genuinely broken links need different fixes.
  • Redirecting every phantom path reflexively — a redirect is for recurring, high-traffic phantoms; blanket wildcard redirects mask real architecture problems.

Where consensus is missing

There is no standard phantom-rate metric and no cross-provider study of how often fabricated URLs carry real brand domains. The term itself circulates informally (phantom citation, hallucinated URL, fabricated source); measurement conventions — resolve-time, redirect handling, treatment of soft-404s — are unstandardized.
