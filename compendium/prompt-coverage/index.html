Prompt Coverage

Prompt Coverage measures the share of the strategically relevant buyer-question space in which a brand achieves any visibility in AI-generated answers. Unlike Share of Voice — which scores relative frequency within an already-tested prompt set — Prompt Coverage answers the prior question: across all the prompts that matter for the buying decision, in how many does the brand appear at all? A denominator question, revealing structural blind spots that Share of Voice cannot see.

Consensus definition

Prompt Coverage is the proportion of prompts in a strategically constructed buyer-journey prompt universe in which a brand receives at least one mention in an AI system's generated response. Formally: (prompts where the brand appears) ÷ (total prompts in the defined set)12. This denominator logic distinguishes it from Share of Voice, which measures relative frequency among mentions within an already-triggered response. The prompt universe is constructed by mapping buyer-journey stages — typically awareness, consideration and purchase/decision — and populating each stage with intent clusters drawn from People Also Ask extracts, community forums, LLM autocomplete and keyword data34. Practical universes range from 20–40 prompts for operational tracking to 200 prompts for research-grade benchmarks across five AI platforms23. The conceptual parent in classic SEO is topical coverage: the share of a topic universe for which a brand holds ranking content, expressed as covered clusters ÷ total target clusters5. Prompt Coverage applies the same logic to conversational AI queries rather than keyword sets, shifting the unit of analysis from index positions to AI-response inclusion.

rhinegold operator refinement

Rhinegold's reframe: Share of Voice over-rotates measurement teams toward what is already in the frame — prompts already in the test set, responses already retrieved, brands already present. The coverage question is upstream. A brand can hold 40 % Share of Voice across the prompts it tracks while being entirely absent from a full buying stage — say, all awareness-phase "what is" and "how do I" queries — simply because those prompts were never tested. Prompt Coverage forces the question of whether the prompt universe itself is complete before any competitive scoring begins.

Operational use

Map buyer-journey stages (awareness, consideration, decision) and define 50–200 prompts per scope by sourcing from People Also Ask, community forums and LLM-native question variations. Run each prompt across target AI platforms (ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Mode at minimum). Compute Coverage Rate = prompts with brand present ÷ total prompts. Segment by stage to identify which funnel phases have structural gaps. Refresh the prompt universe every 30–60 days as buyer language and AI behaviour shift34.

Measurement boundary

The prompt universe is inherently judgment-laden: which questions "count" reflects analyst assumptions about the buyer, and different teams building sets for the same category produce meaningfully different universes. Coverage rates are therefore not directly comparable across vendors unless prompt-construction methodology is disclosed. The universe is non-stationary: buyer questions evolve with category maturity and competitor positioning, requiring refresh cycles rather than a one-time baseline1. A floor of 50 prompts per category is cited as the minimum for statistically reliable estimates1.

Distinct from

From Share of Voice: SoV is a numerator ratio conditioned on being in the frame — it measures the fraction of AI responses that include the brand relative to total category mentions within a fixed tested set. Prompt Coverage asks whether the brand is in the frame at all, across all relevant prompts. From Discovery Prompt: a specific prompt type optimised to trigger awareness-stage discovery — a sub-category used to populate a prompt universe, not a metric over it. From Query Fan-Out: an internal mechanism by which AI systems decompose a single prompt into sub-queries during retrieval; Prompt Coverage is operator-side measurement, agnostic to what happens inside the model. From Mention Rate: often used interchangeably, but Mention Rate sometimes counts total mentions (including multiple per response) while Coverage is a binary presence/absence per prompt — confirm the denominator and counting convention when comparing tools.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing Share of Voice with Coverage: reporting "60 % SoV" without stating what fraction of the relevant prompt universe was tested. High SoV on a narrow prompt set can coexist with near-zero coverage on the full buying-stage space.
  • Under-sampling the awareness stage: practitioners tend to over-index on branded and comparison prompts (easier to justify upward) while omitting the broader "what is" and "how do I" queries where AI shapes category understanding before shortlisting.
  • Treating the prompt universe as static: building a 50-prompt set at project start and running it unchanged for 12 months, while buyer language and AI response patterns shift materially — measured drift between runs can leave the universe substantially out-of-date.
  • Inflating coverage with branded prompts: including direct brand-name queries ("What does [brand] offer?") in the universe inflates Coverage Rate because these almost always return a mention; track them separately, never inside category-coverage denominators.
