Consensus definition

Google describes search engine optimization (SEO) as working on improving a site's presence in Search1. Generative engine optimization (GEO) was introduced by Aggarwal et al. as a black-box optimization framework that helps content creators improve their visibility inside generative engine responses2. The platforms place the two differently. Google states that optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO, and files the labels AEO and GEO under that work3; for AI Overviews and AI Mode it lists no additional requirements, only that a page be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet4. Microsoft uses GEO for the practice of understanding how content takes part in AI-driven answers and says that visibility is no longer defined only by rankings or clicks5. It describes search indexing as built to help humans decide what to read and grounding indexing as being built to help AI systems decide what to say: the infrastructure is shared, but what it takes to do both well is not the same, even from the same foundation6.

rhinegold operator refinement

Rhinegold's position: GEO is SEO with a second set of outcome measures. The access conditions are the same — crawling, indexing and eligibility decide whether a page can be retrieved at all46. The outcome measures are not. Rank describes a page's position for the query a user typed; Mention Rate and Citation Rate describe whether a brand is named or cited inside the answer, across a fixed prompt set. Rank for the typed query raises the odds of a citation but does not secure one: in one count of 168 million AI Overview citations, pages in position one were cited about a quarter of the time and 57% of citations came from outside the organic top 107. The working rule: a GEO claim only holds if mention or citation measures move — by more than their run-to-run spread, and more than on comparable pages left unchanged over the same period. A change that lifts rank and leaves those measures flat is reported as an SEO result. Google's statement that GEO is still SEO3 describes the access conditions; it says nothing about how the outcome is measured.

Operational use

Use this comparison when a programme has to decide whether a change produced an SEO result or a GEO result, and when a vendor sells GEO as a discipline with requirements of its own. Plan the access conditions once: crawlability, indexing, and text that answers the sub-questions a query fans out into. Report two sets of outcome measures: rank and clicks for SEO; Mention Rate and Citation Rate on a fixed discovery-prompt set for GEO, measured repeatedly, with the platform AI-feature reports read alongside but not added to them. Label every result with the measure it moved.

Measurement boundary

Neither set of measures is complete, and they count different things. Mention Rate and Citation Rate count answers in a fixed prompt set; Google's Generative AI performance report counts link impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode together, as part of its Web search data, and counts several results from the same site in one feature as one impression in the chart total8; a brand named without a link does not appear in it. Bing's AI Performance report counts citations shown as sources; Microsoft states that cited-page figures do not indicate ranking, authority or a page's role in an individual answer, and calls Citation Share an observational metric9. Microsoft also writes that it is still learning how to measure grounding quality rigorously6. Answers and citations vary from run to run10, so a single prompt run is not a measurement. What stays out of reach: why a passage was selected and which sub-queries produced a given buyer's answer — for your own test prompts several provider APIs return the searches they ran, see Query Fan-Out.

Distinct from

From Generative Engine Optimization: that entry defines GEO as a practice; this one compares its outcome measures with those of SEO. The access conditions are shared; that does not make the two one discipline, because the outcome measures are not. From the featured snippet: a snippet quotes one page in a fixed position on a ranked results page, so rank tracking can see it; a generated answer draws on several sources, and its citations sit outside the ranked list. From Grounding: grounding is the retrieval step that makes a citation possible; GEO is the work aimed at that outcome. From Query Fan-Out: fan-out is the proposed explanation for why typed-query rank and citation drift apart, not a metric. From Zero-Click Search: zero-click describes searches that end without a click; mention and citation measures count appearances in answers and say nothing about whether anyone clicks.

Common mistakes

  • Reporting a rank gain as a GEO result — unless mention or citation measures moved, it is an SEO result.
  • Assuming that a top-10 rank for the typed query guarantees a citation; it raises the odds, but most cited URLs in AI Overviews rank outside that top 10.
  • Buying GEO as a discipline with its own technical requirements; Google states that its AI features need no special files, markup or optimizations beyond SEO fundamentals.
  • Reading the Search Console Generative AI report as citation share — it counts link impressions for AI Overviews and AI Mode combined, with no clicks.
  • Judging GEO on a single prompt run — answers and citations vary per run, so a fixed prompt set has to be measured repeatedly.
  • Comparing citation-overlap figures across vendors — Ahrefs, Conductor and BrightEdge count different citations against different result sets.

Where consensus is missing

We found no published study that measures the effect of one change on rank and on citation for the same pages over time. The visibility gains reported for GEO methods come from the authors' own benchmark and a check on Perplexity.ai in which source texts were uploaded as files; the authors did not test effects on search rankings2. Ahrefs measured about 76% in July 2025, counting only the three most visible citations per answer, and about 38% in March 2026 after changing its parsing and after Google moved AI Overviews to Gemini 3; the author suggests query fan-out as a reason, which the study does not test10. Other vendors count other citations against other result sets and periods: about 17% of cited sources in the organic top 10 from February to July 202511, about 43% from June to August 20267. The finding on source preference rests on a single preprint12.
